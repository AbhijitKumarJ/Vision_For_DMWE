# Chapter 11: Mechanical & Industrial Design

**Volume III-A — Hardware Design Reference Manual · Working Draft v0.1**

## 11.1 Hardware Must Be Wearable

Mechanical design determines whether modules are adopted. Comfort drives engineering.

## 11.2 Ergonomics

- Fit body curvature and natural movement.
- Minimize pressure points.
- Design for long wear sessions.

## 11.3 Weight Distribution

- Distribute mass for balance (ring vs necklace vs headband).
- Keep heaviest components near support points.
- Inertia affects gesture recognition — document center of mass.

## 11.4 Curved PCBs

- Rigid-flex or conformal designs for curved modules.
- Verify bend radius against component stress.

## 11.5 Flexible PCBs

- Use flex for chassis interconnects and hinge regions.
- Design bend regions without components or vias.
- Strain relief at flex-to-rigid transitions.

## 11.6 Material Selection

- Skin-safe, hypoallergenic materials.
- Corrosion resistance to sweat and cosmetics.
- Durability for impacts and flex.

## 11.7 Comfort

- Surface finish, edge radii, weight, and thermal feel.
- Breathing/venting to reduce heat buildup.
- Adjustability for fit.

## 11.8 Skin Contact

- Electrode materials for bio sensing (EMG/PPG).
- Contact pressure consistent for signal quality.
- Document placement for spatial identity (Volume III, Ch 10).

## 11.9 Ingress Protection

- IPx6 minimum when mated (Volume III, Ch 3).
- Sealing at connector, buttons, acoustic ports.
- Test under sweat and water exposure (Chapter 15).

## 11.10 Ventilation

- Allow heat escape near hot components.
- Acoustic ports for microphones/speakers.
- Keep ventilation away from skin for thermal comfort.

## 11.11 Heat Dissipation

- Conduction to larger thermal mass (chassis).
- Surface area for convection.
- Thermal budgets per body location (Chapter 12).

## 11.12 Modularity

- Serviceable sub-assemblies (battery, sensor, electronics).
- Tool-free battery replacement where possible.
- Hot-swap compatibility with the chassis.

## 11.13 Aesthetics

- Design language consistent with ecosystem.
- Discrete or expressive per user preference.
- Aesthetics MUST NOT compromise sensing or RF.

## 11.14 Summary

Mechanical design is a first-class engineering discipline: comfort, sealing, and modularity determine real-world viability.
